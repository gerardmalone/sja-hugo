## Message from Father Peter

as you can see overleaf, this Friday is the Feast of Saint Ignatius Loyola, founder of the Jesuit Order. Here in our Diocese we still have Jesuit Priests at St. Wilfred’s in Preston, and until not so long ago they occuopied Sacred Heart in Blackpoo1. There have been many famous Jesuits throughout history, including the Poet and Priest, Gerard Manley Hopkins. Currently there is a movement to have Hopkins canonized, though I’m nor sure whereabouts in the process he is. Born at Stratford in Essex on July 28th, 1844, Hopkins is regarded as one the Victorian era's greatest poets, even though none of his poems were actually published in his life time. He was received into the Church by none other than St. John Henry Newman, no slouch in writing religious poetry himself, of course, and after becoming a Jesuit he vowed to ‘write no more...unless it were by the wish of my superiors.’ Hopkins burnt all of the poetry he had written to date and would not write poems again until 1875.  He was ordained Priest in 1877, teaching at Stonyhurst College, just over the Ribble Valley, among other places. In 1884, he became a Professor of Greek at the Royal University College in Dublin, and died five years later from typhoid fever. One of his most famous poems appears in the Divine Office, the Priest’s devotions each day, to be used instead of a hymn, praising God for all creation, and wonder and beauty of nature around us. It’s rather beautiful.
Glory be to God for dappled things -
For skies of couple-colour as a brinded cow;
For rose-moles all in stipple upon trout that swim;
Fresh-firecoal chestnut-falls; finches’ wings;
Landscape plotted and pieced - fold, fallow, and plough;
And all trades, their gear and tackle and trim.
All things counter, original, spare, strange;
Whatever is fickle, freckled (who knows how?)
With swift, slow; sweet, sour; a dazzle, dim;
He fathers-forth whose beauty is past change: Praise Him.

## Feast of St Ignatius Loyola

This is a big feast day for the Jesuits, as St Ignatius was their founder. He was born in 1491 in northern Spain. He worked in the castle and in the army. While he was convalescing after being shot in the leg, he read the lives of the saints. As a result he was converted to a life of holiness. He tried to set up a mission in Palestine, but was thrown out. Back at home he was regarded with suspicion by the Church authorities. Eventually, he studied theology in Rome and later formed the Society of Jesus. The Jesuits put themselves directly under the Pope, becoming known as ‘the Pope's Army’ and did much work in the reform of the Church. Ignatius died in 1556, aged sixty-five.
